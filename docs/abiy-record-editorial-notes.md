# Editorial notes: Ethiopia beyond the skyline

Prepared September 24, 2026 from Daniel Asfaw’s draft and clarification. The essay appears under Daniel Asfaw and Yohannes Asfaw’s byline. It is original opinion, not an original field investigation.

The family passage preserves the supplied account: grandfather had thirteen children; their mother was the first to receive an education instead of being forced into marriage; five of her sisters remained dependent on farming, cattle and other survival work. The account of male relatives’ deaths remains attributed family history, without invented names, dates, numbers or incident findings.

The article’s inline citations and numbered source list are the public evidence record. The following changes keep the argument supportable:

- Population is dated: World Bank’s approximately 135.9 million for 2025, described as approaching 140 million. No claim of a current census or exact rural total.
- Eight to ten children is not asserted as average fertility. UNFPA’s estimated national total fertility rate is distinguished from family size. Literacy is not called “unmeasurable.”
- Child marriage, children leaving school for work and conflict’s effect on education are supported with UNICEF material. No blanket claim that every rural girl marries at fifteen or marries a man twice her age.
- Hospital destruction uses MSF’s defined 2020–2021 Tigray sample, not a national or present-day estimate.
- Agricultural constraints are supported by FAO; useful rural investment is acknowledged with a World Bank project account. Farmers are not described as universally lacking fertilizer or machinery. No claim that manure is intrinsically backward.
- Forced evictions use Amnesty’s April 2025 investigation and its stated sample. Unsubstantiated construction-death cover-ups, bribery and claims of millions spent on IShowSpeed were omitted. The criticism of image-driven development remains.
- The IMF’s 2024 arrangement and UAE’s 2018 aid/investment announcement are separate. Neither demonstrates that those funds paid for specific Addis projects. A Dubai comparison is explicitly the authors’ policy critique.
- The war is dated 2020–2022. Eritrean forces were involved early; their intervention did not begin only after the 2021 Tigrayan advance. Sexual violence is attributed to documented actors, without collective ethnic guilt or invented psychological motives.
- Roughly 600,000 deaths is an attributed, disputed estimate discussed by ACLED, not an established toll. No unverified two-to-six-million displacement range is published.
- Drone procurement is described within the limits of the October 2021 Reuters account. The Dedebit civilian strike has a separate HRW citation. The article does not claim drones were the sole cause of the battlefield reversal or the government's sole present strategy.
- Pretoria is a negotiated cessation-of-hostilities framework, not simply a surrender. Current developments are dated September 2026.
- Unprovable claims about secret ethnic/religious manipulation and paranoia are recast as criticism of public leadership and demands for accountable institutions. No assertion that Egypt funds the new coalition is made.
- Sudan’s present war began April 15, 2023, not 2022. The call for Abiy’s resignation is framed as a peaceful, lawful civilian transition and credible elections, not support for a coup.
